Police constabularies in the UK are starting to use a form of situational
judgement test as part of the assessment criteria for new recruits.
Another name for this type of test is the ‘PSJT’. The PSJT was

one which could possibly be improved upon, via small changes in

Tips for passing the Situational Judgement Test
There are several things you can do in order to help improve your scores
during the PSJT. The following tips will help you to prepare effectively
and improve your overall ability to pass this type of psychometric test:
8
TIP 1 – There are many types of Situational Judgement Tests available
2
on the market. Just because you are applying to become a police
officer, doesn’t mean you should focus solely on police off4icer related
SJTs. Obtain as many SJT questions as possible, from a variety of
5
different genres, and practise accordingly.
0
TIP 2 – When you read the question, you will be presented with a
‘situation’. Read the situation quickly before 0scanning each of the
presented options. Look for the safest option first, and then look for
the option you would be least likely to carDry out at all. Then, assess the
final two options and place them in order of priority based on what you
would do in that type of situation. I
e
TIP 3 – You do not need to be a police officer in order to pass the police
officer related situational judmgement test questions provided within this
book. What you do need to be is professionally conscious, and have
a good level of common sense. When assessing the situation and
o
presented options, always err on the side of caution. Don’t take silly
risks. c
e
TIP 4 – Be entirely honest in how you answer the questions. This is
very importaBnt. The role of a police officer involves a high degree of
trust, integrity, professionalism, safety and a strict adherence to rules
2
and procedures. The test is designed to assess how you would perform
or rewact to given situations. You should also be aware that the police
constabulary may ask you questions during the interview based on
othe answers you provide during the Situational Judgement Test. For
example, if you answer a question during the PSJT that indicates you
H
prefer to work alone as opposed to working as part of a team, yet
you contradict yourself during one of the interview questions and state
that you prefer to work as part of a team, this could lose you marks
and imply you are dishonest. By answering the questions honestly
during the test, you will not have to worry about how you answer the
questions during the interview.
TIP 5 – Preparation is key! In the weeks leading up to your test, work
hard to improve your skills in the testing areas. In addition to the tests
contained within this guide there are numerous other testing resources
